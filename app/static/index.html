    [ AI design review · open-silicon analog ]

    The analog block, reviewed in the chip it lives on.

    Analog blocks get simulated alone, every supply on at once. Chips don't power up that way. Kaaval labels every pin's job and powers each block up the way the chip will: rails in every order and speed, enables and resets early and late (or at your logic's own timing, from a digital simulation's VCD), the output on its pad. Every finding is proven by simulation.

    S.01

    Read

    Every schematic and SPICE netlist in the repository is rebuilt as text, the way xschem would netlist it. Units are checked against the silicon. Nothing in the repository ever runs.

    
  
    

  
    
    
    S.02
    Plan
    Muse Spark reads the whole design, names each block, labels the job of every pin with its evidence, and writes the tests the designer never ran: corners, loops, and the chip's power-up sequences. A plan is data: the engine checks every cell, port and value before anything is simulated.

    S.03
    Prove
    The circuit is powered up from 0 V and held, at every corner, temperature and supply. A second state only counts once the DC solver confirms it; an oscillation only counts if it survives a stiffer integrator.

    S.04
    In the chip
    On a chip the 3.3 V and 1.8 V rails come up in some order, at some speed, and the logic decides when an enable or reset lets go. The block must come up the same way every time. Kaaval powers it up in every order, sweeps the gap between the rails, and names where it starts failing, never one chosen number.
